Bars make bins

7 identical candies to 3 kids. The candies have no names, so "candy 1 goes to A" means nothing. What does have a name is a slot: a row of 9 slots, 7 hold a star (a candy) and 2 hold a bar (a wall between kids).

Every way to place the 2 bars is one handout. So count the ways to pick which 2 of the 9 slots are bars: C(9,2) = 36.

General rule: n identical items into k different bins, empty bins allowed, is C(n + k - 1, k - 1). The n + k - 1 is the slots, the k - 1 is the bars. Guide 4.9.

explain

In your own words: why does placing 2 bars among 7 stars count the handouts? What does one bar stand for?

feedback · "A bar is the line between one kid's pile and the next" is exactly it. One thing to sharpen: you said 2 bars because there are 2 gaps between 3 kids. That is the reason, and it generalizes: k kids always need k - 1 walls.
predict

9 identical candies, 4 kids, any kid may get zero. How many stars, how many bars, how many slots? Write the count as C(_, _) and compute it.

feedback · 9 stars, 3 bars, 12 slots, C(12,3) = 220. Correct, and you wrote the slots before the formula, which is the habit that stops slips.
break it

A classmate says 7 candies to 3 kids is 37, "because each candy picks a kid." Name what 37 counts that we do not want. Give one concrete pair of outcomes it counts as different that are really the same.

feedback · Right: 37 treats the candies as different, so "candy 1 to A, candy 2 to B" and "candy 2 to A, candy 1 to B" count twice, but both are "A gets one, B gets one." Log this one: distinct items means kn, identical items means stars and bars. Guide 4.10 has the 2x2 table.

Identical items into different bins, empty allowed

n items, k bins. Slots = n + k - 1. Bars = k - 1. Count = C(n + k - 1, k - 1).

Glossary

stars and bars
A picture for handing out n identical items to k different bins: n stars and k - 1 bars in a row. Each arrangement is one handout. Avoid: combinations with repetition, multiset formula. Same thing, more words.
bin
A place that receives items and has its own identity: a kid, a box, a variable in a sum. Bins are always distinct in this topic.
identical items
Items you cannot tell apart, so swapping two of them is not a new outcome. Candies, coins, units of a sum. Opposite: distinct items, which give kn.

How this works

  • One page per topic. Lessons are listed in the sidebar in order. A filled dot is passed, a ringed dot is the one to do now, grey is planned.
  • Picture first. Read the picture before the words. The caption points at the one thing to look at.
  • Two kinds of lesson. A concept lesson ends with questions you answer in your own words. The tutor grades those. A drill checks itself as you go, and the tutor reads your attempts afterwards.
  • Your answers save as you type. They sync across your devices when the page runs on claude.ai. The bottom of the sidebar says which.
  • When you finish a lesson, say done in the terminal. Feedback appears under each question, the next lesson unlocks, and the vault note gets the record.
  • Other words the terminal understands: stuck (more help now), too easy or too hard (change the help level), just tell me (get the answer), next (move on).
  • Help level. Each lesson shows one: 3 worked example, 2 outline, 1 bare, 0 you define the check. Two clean lessons in a row moves it down. Getting stuck moves it up.
  • When you are wrong, the tutor first works out why. A wrong mental model gets a picture of where your answer parts from reality and one question. A slip gets a finger pointed at the step. An edge case gets the failing input. You get the answer whenever you ask for it.
  • Cheat sheet and glossary grow as you pass lessons. They hold only things you have already used correctly.
