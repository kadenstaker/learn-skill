reference

How this works

  • One page per topic. Lessons are listed in the sidebar in order. A filled dot is passed, a ringed dot is the one to do now, grey is not written yet.
  • Picture first. Read the picture before the words. The caption points at the one thing to look at.
  • Two kinds of lesson. A concept lesson ends with questions you answer in your own words. The tutor grades those. A drill checks itself as you go, and the tutor reads your attempts afterwards.
  • Your answers save as you type. When the page is hosted by a tool that can store them, they sync across your devices. Opened as a local file, they stay in this browser and a Copy answers button appears at the end of each lesson. The bottom of the sidebar says which.
  • When you finish a lesson, say done in the terminal. On a local page, press Copy answers first and paste them with done. Feedback appears under each question, the next lesson unlocks, and the vault note gets the record.
  • Other words the terminal understands: stuck (more help now), too easy or too hard (change the help level), just tell me (get the answer), next (move on).
  • Help level. Each lesson shows one: 3 worked example, 2 outline, 1 bare, 0 you define the check. Two clean lessons in a row moves it down. Getting stuck moves it up.
  • When you are wrong, the tutor first works out why. A wrong mental model gets a picture of where your answer parts from reality and one question. A slip gets a finger pointed at the step. An edge case gets the failing input. You get the answer whenever you ask for it.
  • The cheat sheet grows as you pass lessons. It holds only rules you have already used correctly.
