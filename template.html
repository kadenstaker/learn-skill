How this works

  • One page per goal. Lessons are listed in the sidebar in order, grouped by topic. A filled dot is passed, a ringed dot is the one to do now, grey is not written yet.
  • Guess first. A concept lesson opens with one question you answer before it teaches anything. You get one try, and a wrong guess costs nothing: it makes the picture that opens next easier to remember. The caption points at the one thing to look at.
  • How sure? Some questions ask first: sure, think so, or guess. Tap one, then answer. It tells the tutor whether a wrong answer was a slip or a belief, and a lucky guess does not count as knowing the rule.
  • Two kinds of lesson. A concept lesson ends with a few questions, at least one in your own words. The tutor grades those. A drill checks itself as you go, and the tutor reads your attempts afterwards. Some items show a worked example with one wrong line to find, or steps to put in order: tap a step to place it next, tap it again to send it back.
  • Your answers save as you type. When the page is served from your computer, or hosted by a tool that can store them, they are saved there and reach your other devices. Opened as a local file, they stay in this browser and a Copy answers button appears at the end of each lesson. The bottom of the sidebar says which. If some answers are not sent yet, it says how many, and Copy answers appears on the lesson.
  • When you finish a lesson, press Finish lesson. The tutor grades finished lessons first thing on its next run: feedback appears under each question, the next lesson unlocks, and the vault note gets the record. Until then the lesson says it is waiting for the tutor. On a local page, Finish lesson also copies a block: paste it in the terminal with done. Saying done on its own works too.
  • The page remembers where you stopped, on every device, and opens there. Back after a few days away, a line at the top says how far you got and which question is next.
  • Other words the terminal understands: stuck (more help now), too easy or too hard (change the help level), just tell me (get the answer), next (move on).
  • Help level. Each lesson shows one: 3 worked example, 2 outline, 1 bare, 0 you define the check. Two clean lessons in a row moves it down. Getting stuck moves it up.
  • When you are wrong, the tutor first works out why. A wrong mental model gets a picture of where your answer parts from reality and one question. A slip gets a finger pointed at the step. An edge case gets the failing input. You get the answer whenever you ask for it.
  • The cheat sheet grows as you pass lessons. It holds only rules you have already used correctly.
  • Review. After you pass a lesson, its rule comes back every few days as a short question with new numbers. One try, then the answer shows. When one is due, the page opens there first; it is never more than three a day. A rule counts as learned once you get it right on three separate days. The dots next to each cheat-sheet rule show how far it got: seen, got it once, got it again on a later day, learned.
  • That's today. Once the review due today is done and you have finished a lesson, the page says so and names the next review day. I want more opens a few extra review items.
  • Progress. The bar under the goal counts passed lessons, and the first lesson once you answer it. The small calendar fills a square for each day you practiced: the day's review done, or 3 questions answered. The streak counts those days. One day off does not end it; after two days off, an extra review set the next day carries it on.
